Build a student poverty index

Arizona unified school districts · American Community Survey 2020–2024

    The task

    Your group is advising the state on which Arizona school districts have the most student need. Most states measure student poverty with free and reduced-price lunch (FRPL), which counts children whose family income is under a cutoff tied to the federal poverty line.

    You will start from that income line and decide whether, and how, to measure need more broadly. You will make four decisions, then compare your ranking with the income-only ranking.

    The income-only ranking uses the share of children ages 6 to 17 in families below 185 percent of the poverty line, the FRPL reduced-price line.

    About 20 minutes. Nothing you enter leaves this browser.

    Step 1. Choose your indicators

    Pick four to six. Each one is a percent, and higher always means more need. What counts as student need for your group?

    Step 2. Set your weights

    Split 100 points among your indicators. A bigger weight means that indicator matters more in your index.

    Step 3. Choose a scaling method

    Your indicators are on different scales. Plumbing runs from 0 to 25 percent while rent burden runs from 0 to 71 percent. Scaling puts them on a common footing before they are added up.

    Scaling method

    Step 4. Draw your line

    What share of districts should count as high need? The same share applies to your index and to the income-only ranking, so the two lists are the same length.
